Merge Point Lab

One highway lane, one on-ramp, the Intelligent Driver Model. The two runs share identical arrivals; only the entrance signal differs. Watch the merge break down at the dashed line, then see what the signal does about it.

00:00

Demand · surge 10–30 min

Entrance signal (right run)

ALINEA: each 30 s, rate += gain × (target − measured density). Critical density for this IDM is 27 veh/km; capacity 1836 veh/h.

Changes to demand or the signal take effect on Reset.

What the model does. Vehicles follow the IDM with Treiber's unstable parameter set (120 km/h desired speed, 1.5 s headway, 0.73 m/s² acceleration, 1.67 m/s² comfortable braking). Ramp vehicles look for a gap along a 250 m acceleration lane and merge if nobody has to brake harder than 3 m/s²; after 6 s of waiting they force their way in. Those forced merges seed the breakdown, and once the merge is congested its discharge drops from 1836 to about 1570 veh/h, so the jam outlives the surge.

What the signal does. A detector 100–400 m past the merge measures density every 30 s. ALINEA raises the release rate while density is below target and cuts it when above, holding the merge just under the critical point so it keeps flowing at capacity. The cost is a queue on the ramp; the payoff is total time spent, which counts ramp waiting too.
